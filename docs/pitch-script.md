# FlexiCure - Pitch Script
### Team FLEXICURE, PrintoCent InnoFest 2026

## Day 1: 3-Minute Pitch

**[0:00-0:20] The problem**

Menstrual cramping and everyday abdominal muscle tension are managed by pure guesswork. A heat wrap or a painkiller, applied on a hunch, with no signal for when tension is actually building, or whether it helped at all. That's not a rare problem. It's a daily one, for a huge number of women.

**[0:20-0:45] Whose problem, and how it's solved today**

Today there are two options, and neither senses anything. Heat wraps and painkillers, fully manual, no data. Or pelvic wellness devices like Elvie and Perifit, which do sense something, but require internal insertion, and are built for exercise and strengthening, a different muscle group, a different job entirely.

**[0:45-1:15] The idea**

FlexiCure is a non-insertable, two-part wearable. A small, rechargeable electronics module, worn continuously, one charge lasting a full week of sensing. It clicks into a low-cost, disposable attachable part, the actual patch, replaced every few days, carrying the printed electrodes and the heating element.

Here's why the split matters: sensing sips power, but a single heat session can use more energy than an entire week of sensing. Put both on one battery and your battery-life claim breaks the first time someone uses heat. Splitting them is what makes a week-long charge actually true.

**[1:15-1:50] The science, and why it's not a guess**

It's worn on the lower abdomen, over the uterus, the exact placement used in electrohysterography, a real clinical technique already trusted to monitor uterine activity in hospitals. We're not inventing new physiology. We're bringing hospital-grade sensing to an everyday problem it was never built for.

And when it senses tension, it responds with heat automatically. That's not a guess either: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial found heat therapy actually outperforms electrical stimulation for menstrual pain, with none of the side effects of daily NSAIDs.

**[1:50-2:20] The app, and why it gets smarter**

The companion app uses one-tap logging, Easy, Manageable, Hard, Severe, no forms, no friction. Every tap personalizes the heat trigger specifically for that user, not a population average. Over time, it starts suggesting heat before tension peaks, based on the person's own pattern.

**[2:20-2:45] The market and the model**

The pelvic wellness market is real and sized: 892 million dollars in 2026, heading toward 1.9 billion by 2035. Elvie and Perifit already prove women will spend 100 to 200 euros here. We reach the same buyer with a non-insertable alternative, and our two-part hardware means our recurring cost is a fraction of theirs, a razor-and-blades model: one module, sold once, cheap patches after that.

**[2:45-3:00] Close**

One reusable brain. One disposable patch. Built on science that already works, priced to actually win. That's FlexiCure.

## Delivery Notes

- **Pace check:** read both scripts aloud once with a timer before presenting. Day 1 targets 3 minutes at a natural conversational pace; Day 2 is written tight to 60 seconds at a confident sales pace, not a rushed one, time it and trim if needed rather than speeding up.
- **Point to the physical patch and puck** while describing the two-part hardware, if you have a prototype or mockup, rather than just talking over slides.
- **Don't state a specific number for the puck's real-world battery life beyond "a week of sensing"** if pressed for exact hours, that number is a design target, not something bench-tested yet, and it's better to say that plainly than guess.
- **If asked "have you tested the Adaptive Comfort Engine,"** the honest answer is no, that's exactly what the pilot phase is for.
- **If asked about TENS integration or labor monitoring**, both are real and grounded, but neither is the product being sold today, be ready to say the current product stays a non-medical wellness device, and those are roadmap directions, not shipped features.
